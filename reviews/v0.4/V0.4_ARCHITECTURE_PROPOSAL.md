# BTC Quant Agent v0.4 Architecture Proposal: Research Kernel & Economic Validation

## 1. Executive Summary

As the quantitative agent evolves beyond the statistical validation boundaries established in v0.3.x (culminating in the frozen v0.3.26 conditional OLS/FWL and required input contract baseline), a structural limitation in the architecture becomes paramount: **statistical predictability does not automatically translate into an economically viable trading strategy**.

A feature may demonstrate statistically significant conditional information (e.g., $p < 0.01$ under Bartlett HAC-robust inference against baseline momentum and volatility controls), yet fail completely as a real-world trading strategy due to:
1. Adverse transaction costs (taker fees and bid-ask spreads dominating marginal returns).
2. Execution latency and market impact (signal degradation between observation and fill).
3. Asymmetric funding cashflows (costly carry during adverse crowding regimes).
4. Inadequate risk budgeting or suboptimal exit timing.

This document establishes the architectural blueprint for **BTC Quant Agent v0.4.0**. It systematically diagnoses existing coupling in the codebase and defines a clean, modular, and reproducible pipeline:

$$\text{Data} \longrightarrow \text{Input Contract} \longrightarrow \text{Information Signal} \longrightarrow \text{Statistical Validation} \longrightarrow \text{Trade Policy} \longrightarrow \text{Economic Simulation} \longrightarrow \text{Qualification}$$

## 2. Current Architecture Coupling Analysis

A rigorous audit of the current codebase (`src/btc_quant_agent/`) reveals four critical coupling bottlenecks:

### 2.1 Research vs. Runtime Coupling
- **Diagnosis**: 
  - Modules such as `microstructure_research.py` directly query operational forward stores (`data/forward/BTCUSDT/microstructure/`), bind to runtime ledger paths, and reference collector internals.
  - The research registry (`research_registry.py`) conflates research verification states (`VALIDATED_FORWARD`, `CANDIDATE`, `FROZEN`) with runtime permissions (`LIVE_ELIGIBLE`, `PAPER_TESTNET_ONLY`, `SHADOW_ONLY`) within a single entity (`RegistryComponent`).
  - Runtime execution components (`execution/service.py`) directly check research registry entries rather than receiving standalone, decoupled trading policies.
- **Architectural Risk**:
  - Research experiments cannot be independently modified or simulated without risking regressions in runtime state machines.
  - Inadvertent leaks between operational data ingestion and research evaluation boundaries.

### 2.2 Signal vs. Policy Coupling
- **Diagnosis**:
  - In `domain.py`, the `Signal` dataclass represents both an informational event and an execution order:
    ```python
    @dataclass(frozen=True)
    class Signal:
        signal_id: str
        direction: Direction
        entry_low: float
        entry_high: float
        stop_loss: float
        take_profit: float
        recommended_notional: float
        estimated_fee_usdt: float
        estimated_slippage_usdt: float
        ...
    ```
  - This design forces an informational insight (e.g. "order flow imbalance indicates positive 60m drift") to prematurely declare entry bands, fixed stop loss / take profit prices, notional allocation, and estimated transaction costs.
- **Architectural Risk**:
  - Prevents the separation of **what is predicted** from **how it is traded**.
  - A single information signal cannot be evaluated across multiple trade policies (e.g., trend following vs. mean-reverting limit orders, fixed time exits vs. volatility trailing stops, risk-parity vs. fixed-fraction sizing).
  - Statistical quality of the predictor cannot be evaluated independently of the heuristics chosen for execution brackets.

### 2.3 Backtest vs. Shadow Execution Semantics
- **Diagnosis**:
  - In `backtest.py` (`resolve_signal`), execution relies on an idealized **"price-touch-equals-fill"** assumption:
    ```python
    if not (bar.low <= signal.entry_high and bar.high >= signal.entry_low):
        continue
    entry_price = signal.entry_high if signal.direction == Direction.LONG else signal.entry_low
    entered_at = bar.open_time_ms
    ```
  - If a price bar's high/low touches the entry threshold, an instantaneous fill is assumed at the boundary price at the bar's open time, ignoring:
    1. Decision delay (time required to compute features and generate orders).
    2. Queue priority on the exchange order book (especially for limit orders).
    3. Slippage and market impact under varying liquidity regimes.
    4. Partial fills and order cancellation/expiry.
  - `shadow.py` merely wraps `backtest.py:resolve_signal`, propagating this identical naive simulation into forward shadow monitoring.
- **Architectural Risk**:
  - Substantial positive survivorship and execution bias in simulated returns. Strategies that exploit microscopic quote touches appear highly profitable in backtests but fail in live execution.

### 2.4 Missing Unified Cost & Cashflow Accounting Model
- **Diagnosis**:
  - Transaction costs are approximated as fixed dollar deductions (`estimated_fee_usdt`, `estimated_slippage_usdt`) attached to signals prior to trade realization.
  - Funding fees are calculated after trade completion in `_funding_cashflow` as an isolated arithmetic adjustment rather than an evolving balance cashflow.
  - There is no continuous portfolio state tracking cash balance, margin requirements, mark-to-market unrealized PnL, or cumulative drawdown.
- **Architectural Risk**:
  - Inability to model cash drag, margin liquidation, compounding, or realistic capital constraints.
  - Inability to compare strategies under varying fee tiers (e.g., VIP maker discounts vs. standard taker penalties).

## 3. Target v0.4 Architecture Specification

To resolve these structural bottlenecks, v0.4 establishes seven strictly decoupled stages:

```
[ Market Data ] 
       │
       ▼
[ Input Contract ] ────────► Causal completeness, timestamp alignment, zero lookahead
       │
       ▼
[ Information Signal ] ────► Pure directional / magnitude forecast (zero execution baggage)
       │
       ▼
[ Statistical Validation ] ─► FWL conditional OLS, Bartlett HAC, FWER control
       │
       ▼
[ Trade Policy ] ──────────► Sizing, entry rules, exit rules, risk budget, market states
       │
       ▼
[ Economic Simulation ] ───► Distinct signal/order/fill timing, fee model, funding cashflow
       │
       ▼
[ Qualification Decision ] ─► Hurdle: Information Edge + Net Economic Return > Benchmarks
```

### Stage 1: Data
- Ingests immutable historical market inputs (canonical 1m OHLCV bars, L2 depth snapshots, aggregated trade streams, PIT funding rates).

### Stage 2: Input Contract (`research_contract/`)
- Enforces explicit mathematical and structural data contracts before feature computation:
  - Strict point-in-time causality ($t \le t_{\text{decision}}$).
  - Minimum causal warmup (e.g. 500 completed 15m bars for ATR calculation).
  - Gap and completeness verification (head, internal, and tail).

### Stage 3: Information Signal (`economic/signal.py`)
- Represents an unadorned statistical prediction:
  - `signal_id`: Unique identifier.
  - `timestamp_ms`: Point-in-time observation timestamp.
  - `asset`: Trading instrument (e.g. `BTCUSDT`).
  - `direction`: Directional expectation (`LONG`, `SHORT`, `NEUTRAL`).
  - `strength`: Continuous normalized prediction (e.g., z-score, return forecast).
  - `confidence_interval`: Lower and upper bounds of forecast.
  - `horizon_ms`: Intended information lifespan.
- **Inviolate Rule**: An `InformationSignal` contains **zero** order parameters (no stop loss, no take profit, no notional size, no execution price).

### Stage 4: Statistical Validation
- Verifies that the information signal contains genuine incremental information beyond existing frozen controls (e.g. trailing returns, ATR ratio).
- Governed by unpenalized FWL conditional regression and Bartlett HAC long-run variance (frozen in v0.3.26).

### Stage 5: Trade Policy (`economic/policy.py`)
- Maps pure information signals into actionable simulated orders:
  - `entry_rule`: Trigger condition, order type (`MARKET` vs. `LIMIT`), price offset, time-in-force.
  - `exit_rule`: Profit target, protective stop, trailing stop, volatility decay exit, signal reversal exit.
  - `holding_period`: Maximum holding duration before mandatory time exit.
  - `position_size`: Capital allocation logic (`FIXED_NOTIONAL`, `VOLATILITY_SCALED`, `RISK_FRACTION`).
  - `risk_budget`: Maximum gross and net portfolio exposure, drawdown thresholds.
  - `allowed_market_state`: Volatility bands, regime prerequisites, spread thresholds.

### Stage 6: Economic Simulation (`economic/simulator.py`, `fee_model.py`, `execution_model.py`, `funding.py`)
- **Strict Timestamp Disaggregation**:
  Every trade records four sequential timestamps:
  $$t_{\text{signal}} \le t_{\text{order}} \le t_{\text{fill}} \le t_{\text{exit}}$$
  where:
  - $t_{\text{signal}}$: Instant the signal was generated.
  - $t_{\text{order}} = t_{\text{signal}} + \Delta t_{\text{decision}}$ (processing latency).
  - $t_{\text{fill}} = t_{\text{order}} + \Delta t_{\text{network}}$ (exchange matching and fill).
  - $P_{\text{fill}}$: Effective execution price including spread and slippage function $S(\text{size}, \sigma)$.
- **Fee Modeling**:
  - Parameterized maker/taker schedules.
  - Volume-dependent slippage penalties.
- **Funding Cashflows**:
  - Exact periodic settlement (every 8 hours at 00:00, 08:00, 16:00 UTC) with continuous position holding proration.
- **Unified Trade Ledger (`TradeEvent`)**:
  - Emits immutable event records tracking timestamp, action, price, quantity, fee, funding, post-trade position, and post-trade cash balance.

### Stage 7: Qualification Decision
- An alpha hypothesis is qualified **only if** it satisfies two joint criteria:
  1. **Statistical Criterion**: Proven incremental conditional information ($p < 0.05$ under FWER-controlled Bartlett HAC).
  2. **Economic Criterion**: Net PnL after all fees, slippage, and funding exceeds:
     - Benchmark A: Capital preservation (Cash / No Trade).
     - Benchmark B: Passive BTC buy-and-hold exposure.
     - Benchmark C: Randomized entry baseline with identical holding durations.

## 4. Legacy Compatibility & Preservation

1. **Historical Immutability**: All existing historical code (`backtest.py`, `shadow.py`, `microstructure_research.py`, `h39_*.py`) and deliverables in `deliverables/v0.3.x/` remain 100% untouched and reproducible.
2. **Namespace Isolation**:
   - New research contract framework resides under `src/btc_quant_agent/research_contract/`.
   - New economic validation and portfolio simulation framework resides under `src/btc_quant_agent/economic/`.
3. **Safety Firewalls**:
   - Zero access to live exchange trading keys.
   - Zero access to forward return labels during feature evaluation.
   - Zero modifications to H39 one-shot blind ledger or unblind prerequisites.
