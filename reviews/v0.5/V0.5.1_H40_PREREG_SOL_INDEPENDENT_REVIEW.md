## H40R-01 — MEDIUM: first candidate-lock partition is not uniquely specified

Section 8 defines four fold-specific training/calibration/validation schedules. Section 12 states that `H40-DISCOVERY -> H40-CANDIDATE_LOCKED` may use training plus a dedicated calibration interval, but it does not identify which one is the sole pre-validation configuration-selection boundary.

This matters because later calibration intervals overlap calendar time that served as earlier validation. For example, WF2 calibration (`2023-05-01` to `2023-07-31`) lies inside WF1 validation (`2023-02-01` to `2023-07-31`). Such reuse is valid for a chronological mechanical refit after time has advanced, but it is not valid if those later calibration outcomes are allowed to influence the original 168-configuration winner, thresholds, asset scope, horizon, family choice, or first candidate lock.

Minimum closure:

1. Define exactly one pre-validation discovery/selection boundary for choosing the sole H40 V1 candidate.
2. The recommended binding is WF1 only:
   - fitting/search training: `2021-01-31T00:00:00Z` to `2022-10-31T00:00:00Z`;
   - purge: `2022-10-31` to `2022-11-01`;
   - calibration: `2022-11-01T00:00:00Z` to `2023-01-31T00:00:00Z`;
   - purge: `2023-01-31` to `2023-02-01`;
   - no WF1 validation or later-fold calibration/validation outcome may influence candidate identity.
3. After candidate lock, WF2-WF4 may mechanically refit/recalibrate using their preregistered historical windows because those observations are past at each simulated decision boundary, but feature semantics, hyperparameters, thresholds, family, asset scope, primary horizon and gate order remain immutable.
4. Failure in walk-forward validation cannot cause selection of the second-best configuration under the same H40 V1 confirmation partition.

Without this explicit boundary, an implementation can legitimately interpret the current text in more than one way, creating outcome-dependent research freedom.

## H40R-02 — MEDIUM: dependence-adjusted `n_eff` is declared frozen but its HAC estimator is not defined

Section 9 defines:

```text
n_eff = min(
  accepted trade count,
  number of distinct occupied UTC 24h outcome blocks,
  HAC effective sample size clipped to [1, accepted trade count]
)
```

but the exact HAC effective-sample-size formula is absent. The text freezes only a maximum lag of 24 hours. Different legitimate choices for autocorrelation estimator, Bartlett weighting, sparse accepted-trade indexing, simultaneous BTC/ETH rows, constant series, missing lags and clipping can materially change whether the Section 11 sample floors pass.

Minimum closure before any outcome computation:

1. Write the exact HAC `n_eff` formula, including the series to which it is applied.
2. Specify indexing on the hourly UTC grid versus accepted-trade order.
3. Specify simultaneous BTC/ETH grouping.
4. Specify Bartlett/kernel weights, denominator convention and lag truncation.
5. Define behavior for zero variance, insufficient lags, missing rows and non-finite estimates.
6. Define whether multiple primary outcome series produce separate effective sizes or a conservative minimum.
7. Persist enough statistics to reconstruct the value exactly.

The repair must not choose a formula after seeing H40 results.

## H40R-03 — LOW but must be frozen with R1: bootstrap and null edge semantics

The moving-block bootstrap and empirical base-rate null are directionally specified but still leave implementation choices that should be eliminated now while no H40 outcome has been computed.

The amendment should define:

- the exact 168-hour moving-block start universe;
- whether blocks are overlapping/circular and how the final partial draw is trimmed;
- how gaps and pooled BTC/ETH rows are carried through a sampled UTC block;
- how a replicate with insufficient LONG/SHORT accepted rows is treated;
- the exact quantile convention used for one-sided 95% / 97.5% lower bounds;
- the formula for the empirical side-mix null `p0`, including which training partition supplies label base rates and which partition supplies the candidate's emitted LONG/SHORT weights;
- the relationship between simultaneous/max-statistic confidence bounds and Holm-adjusted p-values used in candidate selection.

These are not new hypotheses; they are deterministic statistical execution semantics.
