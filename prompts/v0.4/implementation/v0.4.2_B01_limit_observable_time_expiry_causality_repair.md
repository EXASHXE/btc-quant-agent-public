## 3. Exact defect to repair

Current problematic class:

A LIMIT order is submitted at or before a bar open. The bar later has an OHLC low/high proving the limit was touched somewhere during the interval. Existing logic can use that completed-bar extremum and stamp the fill at the **bar open timestamp**, even though the touch may have occurred after:

- the order's expiry/TIF;
- a funding event;
- a stop/target/terminal boundary;
- other causally material events.

Canonical adversarial shape from P7:

```text
bar interval: [T_open, T_close]
open = 100
low = 90
high = 110
close = 100

BUY LIMIT = 95
submitted at T_open
expires at T_open + 1 ms
```

The full 15m candle only establishes that price reached <= 95 **at some unknown time in the bar**. It does not establish that this happened before expiry. Therefore the implementation MUST NOT fabricate a fill at `T_open` from the later low.

The same defect must be treated symmetrically for BUY/SELL LIMIT and touch/through cases.

---

## 4. Required causal model

B01 must distinguish at least these two semantically different situations.

### 4.1 Observable marketable-at-open LIMIT

At the first causally observable price for the order, the order is already marketable.

Examples:

```text
BUY LIMIT L, observable open <= L
SELL LIMIT L, observable open >= L
```

This condition is known at the open itself. Filling at that observable timestamp is permitted, subject to the existing execution/cost/risk model and expiry validity.

This is the positive behavior represented by `G10-MARKETABLE-LIMIT-AT-OPEN`.

Do not break it.

### 4.2 Unresolved intrabar touch inferred only from OHLC extrema

Example:

```text
BUY LIMIT 95
open 100
low 90
```

The low proves a touch occurred somewhere after/open-to-close, but the exact touch time is not observable from OHLC.

Likewise:

```text
SELL LIMIT 105
open 100
high 110
```

The high proves a touch somewhere in the bar but not its ordering relative to expiry/funding/other events.

The engine must not backdate such a touch to bar open.

When intrabar ordering is materially ambiguous, the formal semantic result must be fail-closed. Use the existing project's typed ambiguous/rejected/not-testable mechanism if one exists and fits the execution API. Do not invent a profitable or favorable timestamp.

A generic rule of "always fill at bar close" is NOT sufficient because expiry may occur before close and funding/terminal ordering may still be unknown.

## 5. Required behavior by timing case

Implement and test the following contract.

### Case A — marketable at observable open and not expired

```text
order arrival <= open observable timestamp
BUY LIMIT >= open, or SELL LIMIT <= open
expiry >= executable timestamp
```

Expected:

- may execute at the causally observable open according to declared existing execution semantics;
- no future high/low is required to authorize the fill;
- later-bar suffix changes must not change whether the opening marketability condition existed.

### Case B — not marketable at open; only full-bar extremum proves touch

If no causally timestamped lower-resolution evidence establishes when the touch occurred, do not stamp the fill at open.

If ordering versus expiry/funding/other material event is unresolved, the result must fail closed as ambiguous/not-testable/rejected according to the existing API contract.

### Case C — order expires before unresolved touch can be proven

Example TIF = 1 ms and only completed-bar low/high indicates touch.

Expected: MUST NOT fill.

### Case D — funding lies inside unresolved touch interval

If a touch could have happened before or after funding but OHLC cannot distinguish the paths, the engine must not choose the favorable path or create exposure before funding.

Expected: fail closed for the ambiguous execution path; do not fabricate funding entitlement/payment.

### Case E — order arrives after bar open

Preserve prior causal behavior: an order must not consume earlier extrema from the same bar that occurred before arrival.

### Case F — bar gap/open immediately crosses a limit

If the observable opening price itself makes the limit marketable, this is not ambiguous. Preserve the open-executable semantics.
